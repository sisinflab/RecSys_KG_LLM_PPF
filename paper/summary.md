# Paper summary

## Title
From Side Information and Knowledge Graphs to Large Language Models: Two Decades of Knowledge Integration in Recommender Systems

## One-paragraph summary
The paper reads the history of recommender systems as a sequence of representational translations rather than a sequence of replacements. Early systems relied on explicit constraints, rules, and knowledge engineering; later work translated knowledge into side information, feature spaces, embeddings, linked open data, and knowledge graphs; the latest phase shifts knowledge integration into natural language through LLMs. The central claim is that LLMs should not be treated as substitutes for structured knowledge. They are better understood as a highly expressive interaction layer that must remain grounded in explicit knowledge stores to preserve traceability, controllability, and accountable evaluation.

## Main contributions

1. A compact lens for reading RecSys history as **knowledge integration through representational translation**.
2. A synthesis of **recurring design patterns** that reappear across rule-based, side-information, KG, and LLM-based eras.
3. A cautious future agenda focused on **hybrid, grounded, inspectable, and accountable** recommender systems.
